Tucked into the south-western corner of New Zealand lies Fiordland — a labyrinth of deep inlets, rainforest and glacier-carved rock that has stood apart from the rest of the world for some 85 million years. *A Forest's Dream* steps inside that isolation and tells its story from an unusual point of view: that of the land itself. Water, Rock and Tree — the ancient elements that shaped this place — narrate the dream of their home and the creatures that came to depend on it.

Few places have evolved so freely from large ground predators, and the results are extraordinary: penguins that nest in the forest, parrots that thrive in the snow, and a flightless bird, the Takahē, that came back from the brink of extinction. The film follows the Whio, the Kiwi, the Kea, the Tawaki and the Takahē as their world is upturned — first by geological turmoil, then, far more profoundly, by the arrival of humans and the predators that came with them.

I wanted to make a film that looked at conservation not as a return to some untouched past, but as a relationship — people and nature learning to live together. Its closing thought borrows from Emma Marris' idea of a [rambunctious garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rambunctious_Garden){:target="_blank"}: a future that is green and thriving even where it isn't perfectly pristine. *A Forest's Dream* is my attempt to imagine that future for Fiordland.
